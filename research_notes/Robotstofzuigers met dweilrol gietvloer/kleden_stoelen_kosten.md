# Rolmop-robots in de praktijk: kleden, stoelen, 120 m² en kosten (NL, september 2026)

Scope: roller-mop (dweilrol) robots: Ecovacs Deebot X11 (Pro Omni / OmniCyclone), X8/X9/T90 (OZMO Roller), Narwal Flow / Flow 2, Dreame Aqua10 Ultra Roller (Complete), Roborock Qrevo Curv 2 Flow, Eufy Omni S1 Pro / E28 / S2, Mova Z60 Ultra Roller, SwitchBot S20. Situatie: 120 m² gietvloer (keuken, woonkamer, zitkamer) met eetkamerstoelen en meerdere kleden.
Research date: 30 Sept 2026. Most review data are from 2025 to mid-2026 reviews; US review sites (Vacuum Wars) use US model variants. Tool budget was limited, so some models (Roborock Saros roller, Ecovacs X8/X9 details, Eufy E28, Mova NL pricing) are covered thinly. See Gaps.

## 1. Kleden (rugs): carpet detection, mop lift, roller shield, franjes, dunne kleden

### Takeaway
None of the roller-mop robots detach the roller in the station. They all rely on lifting the roller (10–15 mm, Eufy S2 claimed 28 mm), and some add a motorised shield/cover (Dreame AutoSeal, Roborock Roller Shield, Mova shield) to keep rugs dry. With several rugs, Dreame Aqua10 (14 mm + AutoSeal), Roborock Curv 2 Flow (15 mm + shield) and Mova Z60 (shield) protect them best. Narwal Flow/Flow 2 (10 mm lift, no shield) is weakest on thicker rugs. Thin light rugs and rugs with fringes remain a known weak point for all of them, and nobody offers a dedicated "fringe mode". Users work around it with no-go zones, virtual walls or by tucking fringes under.

### Inferences
- For kitchen/living-room rugs with low to medium pile (≤ ~15 mm), a 14–15 mm lift plus shield (Dreame Aqua10, Roborock Curv 2 Flow, Mova Z60) is the safest option against damp rug edges. On Narwal's 10 mm lift the roller will likely touch or wet thicker rugs. For high-pile rugs (> ~15–20 mm), no roller model avoids contact, so set those rugs to "avoid" or "vacuum only in a separate run".
- Light, thin rugs without anti-slip backing are a known cause of getting stuck (Ecovacs, Eufy). In practice: an anti-slip underlay or no-go zone. Fringes: no-go strip or virtual wall along the fringe edges.
- Carpet modes: all apps let you choose between avoiding the rug, crossing it with the mop lifted, or vacuuming it separately (mentioned in general terms). Per-brand naming of these modes was not verified in detail. Carpet boost (automatic suction increase on carpet) is a standard feature but was not specifically verified per model here.

## 2. Stoelen en meubels: navigatie tussen stoelpoten, obstakeldetectie, hoogte

### Takeaway
All models use LiDAR navigation plus camera and/or structured light. In the Vacuum Wars obstacle test (24 objects), Dreame Aqua10 (21/24), Ecovacs X11 (20/24) and Narwal Flow 2 (20/24) score best. Roborock Curv 2 Flow (16/24) and Narwal Flow (18/24) are average. For dining chairs the robot's height and width matter most. The Roborock Curv 2 Flow is tall at 11.9 cm with a fixed LiDAR tower, and the Dreame Aqua10 has a retractable LiDAR. Nobody tests "chair-leg forests" specifically. Standard advice is to put chairs on the table or pushed in, or to set a no-go zone or separate zone.

### Inferences
- Eetkamerstoelen: roller-mop robots are roughly round/square at 33–35 cm wide (Roborock 35.3 cm). Chair-leg gaps are often narrower, so under the dining table the robot mainly cleans between the chairs, not between every leg. Chairs on the table or pushed under it gives the best result. Alternatively, a separate "dining area" zone with a higher intensity or second pass.
- A camera combined with structured light (Dreame, Ecovacs, Narwal Flow 2) generally scores higher on small obstacles than a camera alone (Roborock "Reactive AI" 16/24). For chair legs (large, fixed objects) LiDAR and bumpers are usually enough. The difference shows mainly with cables and socks.
- Low furniture: for a sofa or cabinet with 10–11 cm clearance, the Roborock Curv 2 Flow (11.9 cm) won't fit. Ecovacs X11 (~10 cm) or Dreame Aqua10 (retractable LiDAR) have a better chance.

## 3. 120 m² in één run: accu, water, zones, planning

### Takeaway
120 m² vacuum+mop in a single charge is realistic for Ecovacs X11 (very efficient, plus PowerBoost fast charging during roller washes), Narwal Flow (50 m² used about 30% battery) and roughly Roborock Curv 2 Flow (< ~121 m² per charge according to Vacuum Wars). Dreame Aqua10 achieved only about 64–69 m² per charge in tests, so for 120 m² it will recharge and resume. All roller models rinse the roller continuously on board, so the mid-run returns are mainly for battery and dirty-water tank, not for washing pads. Per-room settings (e.g. more water in the kitchen) are available in the apps, though that was confirmed only in general terms here.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- 120 m² with chairs and rugs (effective floor area maybe ~90–100 m²): Ecovacs X11 and Narwal Flow manage it in one charge. Roborock Curv 2 Flow is borderline, especially with a second pass in the kitchen. Dreame Aqua10 needs a mid-run recharge, which adds substantial time given the 0.62 m²/min speed and a 1 hr+ total runtime.
- Practical plan: a daily schedule "kitchen + dining area vacuum+mop with higher water level/second pass", and 2–3× per week the whole ground floor. This keeps each run under one battery charge even on the Dreame.
- Estimated time: at ~0.6–0.75 m²/min (Vacuum Wars averages), 100 m² of effective floor takes roughly 2¼–2¾ hours of vacuum+mop, excluding recharging. This is an extrapolation, not a measurement.

## 4. Kosten van bezit, garantie, service, software, privacy, smart home (NL)

### Takeaway
Roller replacements are relatively cheap: an Ecovacs X11 roller costs €19 from the official NL store, and third-party Dreame Aqua10 rollers cost €22.50 for 3. Full maintenance kits run €33–€190 depending on brand and source. The manufacturer warranty is the legal 2 years (Roborock, Dreame, Ecovacs), with service handled directly by the brand through email in the Benelux. Independent repair is available in NL (robotspecialist.nl). For the hot-water and hot-air station, expect roughly 25–80 kWh per year on top of the robot; Narwal Flow was measured at about 12 kWh per month. Home Assistant: Roborock and Ecovacs are in core with area cleaning, Dreame goes through the community HACS integration, and SwitchBot S20 supports Matter. No solid source was found on Narwal HA/Matter support.

### Inferences
- Indicative annual consumables at a normal household intensity (1 roller, 2 filters, 4–6 dust bags, 2 side brushes, 1 main brush per year): about €50–€100 with third-party kits, about €100–€150 with original parts, plus detergent. This is an estimate built from the kit prices above, not a manufacturer figure.
- Station energy: Narwal's measured 12 kWh/month is about 144 kWh/yr. At a Dutch all-in tariff of about €0.25–0.30/kWh (assumption, not sourced here) that is roughly €35–45 per year. Turning off or shortening hot-air drying saves the most.
- Service: all brands work via email or RMA directly with the manufacturer. Buying from Coolblue or Bol mainly adds a local point of contact and return window, plus Coolblue's own service handling, which was not verified here. For an Ecovacs X11, an official €19 roller is the cheapest known option.
